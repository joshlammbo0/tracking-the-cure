How to read it

Displaced revenue for a company = TTM revenue × share of revenue in each market × that market's growth to the year × the T1D share of that market × share of T1D patients cured (× one minus residual CGM use, for CGM). Comparing a future displaced amount to today's revenue is deliberate: it is the simplest way to show scale without forecasting each company.

What it leaves out

Share shifts between competitors, pricing, new T2D and OTC demand, and the cure's own revenue for companies that sell it (Vertex, Sana). Revenue splits are disclosed, derived from filings, or labelled assumptions; Lilly's insulin revenue is not broken out in the primary sources reviewed, so it is shown as unknown rather than estimated.

Methodology

How the model works

Mechanics

  1. Baseline markets. Each market grows from its 2025 size at its CAGR: size × (1 + CAGR)year − 2025.
  2. Demand. From the approval year, target patients = peak share × a smooth S-curve that reaches 1 after the ramp × the global T1D population ({{ market.population.t1d_global_m }}M).
  3. Capacity. Cumulative patients treated can rise by at most the treatment capacity each year. Early on this is usually the binding constraint.
  4. Displacement. Share cured × the T1D share of each market's revenue. For CGM it is further reduced by the share of cured patients assumed to keep a sensor.
  5. Cure sales. Newly treated patients × the one-time price.

Limitations

The model holds the T1D population flat (no new diagnoses or mortality), uses one global blended price, ignores payer budget caps and geographic rollout, and treats each market's T1D share as constant over time. Read it as an order-of-magnitude framework for comparing scenarios, not a forecast.

The same formulas run in Python (which prints every number on this site and writes the CSV) and in the browser (for the sliders). Automated tests pin the Python results.
