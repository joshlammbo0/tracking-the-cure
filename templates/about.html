I was diagnosed with type 1 diabetes in May 2026. Almost immediately, I became interested in a question that felt both personal and financial: how close are we to a functional cure, and what would that mean for the companies whose products help people manage the disease today?

As a finance student at Georgetown University, I've always been interested in understanding how businesses operate, how industries evolve, and how technological developments influence company valuations. My diagnosis gave me an opportunity to explore those questions through an industry that suddenly had a direct impact on my life.

I built Tracking the Cure to bring together clinical research, financial modeling, and industry analysis in one place.

The goal is to evaluate emerging therapies based on the evidence supporting them, while examining how scientific breakthroughs could reshape existing markets and business models.

Method in brief

  • Two grades per program. Stage reached versus evidence read out. The gap is where hype lives.
  • Live checks. ClinicalTrials.gov, openFDA, SEC EDGAR, PubMed and market data refresh daily. Disagreements with the curated record raise a flag.
  • A transparent model. The Cure Impact Model sizes displaced CGM, pump and insulin revenue under labelled assumptions, with sensitivities and a company read-across.
  • Sourced or labelled. Every fact cites a primary source; every judgment is labelled as an assumption, analysis or my take.
