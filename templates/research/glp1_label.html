    GLP-1-class drugs such as semaglutide and tirzepatide are approved for type 2 diabetes and weight management, and some people with type 1 diabetes already take them off-label to lose weight and cut insulin doses. Yet none of the {{ L|length }} brands this site checks every day (Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound, Victoza, Trulicity, Rybelsus) carries a type 1 indication.1 The reason is not that nobody tried.

    The trial that set the bar

    In 2016 Novo Nordisk reported two Phase 3 trials of liraglutide added to insulin in type 1 diabetes. ADJUNCT ONE enrolled {{ a1.enrollment|num }} patients for 52 weeks; ADJUNCT TWO enrolled {{ a2.enrollment|num }}.2,3 Glycemic benefit was modest, reported in the abstracts as small HbA1c reductions versus placebo (this site still flags that summary range for re-checking against the dose-specific tables). At the highest dose, symptomatic hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia with ketosis rose. For regulators, a drug that adds ketosis risk in a population already exposed to diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) needs a large benefit to justify it. Liraglutide did not show one, and no T1D label followed.

    What is different now

    Two things have changed: the drugs under study are newer, and the outcomes people care about have shifted from HbA1c alone to weight, time in range and insulin dose.

    • Tirzepatide. An independent Phase 2 in 24 adults with T1D and obesity reported −8.7 kg body weight and −35.1% total daily insulin versus placebo over 12 weeks, with a borderline HbA1c difference (−0.4 points, P = 0.05) and no severe hypoglycemia or DKA reported.4
    • Semaglutide. ADJUST-T1D randomized 72 adults on automated insulin delivery. After 26 weeks, 36% on semaglutide versus 0% on placebo met a composite of time in range above 70%, time below range under 4% and at least 5% weight loss; time in range rose 8.8 points. There were two severe hypoglycemia events in each arm and no DKA.5

    Both are encouraging, and both are far too small and short to answer the safety question that stopped liraglutide.

    The path to a label

    Lilly is now running SURPASS-T1D-1 (NCT06914895, {{ s1.enrollment|num }} patients, registry status: {{ s1.status|status|lower }}) and SURPASS-T1D-2 (NCT06962280, {{ s2.enrollment|num }} patients), the first industry-sponsored Phase 3 program of a GLP-1-class drug in type 1 diabetes since ADJUNCT.6 The registry estimates primary completion for both in {{ s1.primary_completion|fdate("month") if s1.primary_completion else "a month not yet disclosed" }}. That is when the last patient finishes the primary endpoint, not when results are published; toplines usually follow months later. Novo Nordisk has not announced a semaglutide T1D registration program; the semaglutide trials tracked here are academic.

    The question for a filing is whether DKA and severe hypoglycemia stay flat with hundreds of patients over a longer period. Continuous ketone monitoring could help manage that risk: Abbott's FreeStyle Libre Duo, the first integrated glucose-ketone sensor, received FDA De Novo authorization in August 2026.7

    Why it matters to investors

    A T1D label would mostly change who pays, not who uses: off-label use already exists. For Lilly the T1D population is small next to type 2 diabetes and obesity. The more interesting read-across is to insulin pump and AID makers: if patients need around a third less insulin, pump volumes don't change but insulin consumption does. That would matter to insulin sellers more than to device makers.
