    Insulin pumps and automated insulin delivery (AID) systems exist because the body can no longer make insulin. A functional cure would remove that need entirely, which makes pump makers the cleanest test of what the cure race means for public-market investors. This note runs the Cure Impact Model through the companies tracked on this site.

    Method in one paragraph

    For each company, displaced revenue = current trailing-twelve-month revenue × its share of revenue in each modelled market × that market's growth × the T1D share of the market × the share of T1D patients cured (reduced for CGM by the share of cured patients assumed to keep a sensor). The company is assumed to hold its market share. The result is compared with today's revenue to show scale without forecasting each company. Revenue splits are disclosed, derived from filings, or labelled assumptions; see the read-across table for each basis.

    Three observations

    1. Exposure, not size, drives the ranking. Abbott's Diabetes Care business (≈$8.0B in FY2025) is bigger than any pump maker, but it is about 18% of Abbott's sales, and CGM is partly protected by type 2, over-the-counter use and post-cure monitoring. A pump pure-play has none of those cushions in the model. In the base case its 2040 hit is {{ (podd.years["2040"].pct_current_rev / dxcm.years["2040"].pct_current_rev)|round(1) }}× Dexcom's as a share of current revenue.

    2. Capacity sets the pace. The base case caps treatment at {{ bi.cap|num }} patients a year. With about {{ market.population.t1d_global_m }} million people living with T1D, even a perfect product cures only {{ R.base.summary["2040"].penetration|pct(1) }} of them by 2040. Capacity, more than efficacy, is what holds the base case at {{ podd.years["2040"].pct_current_rev|pct(1) }} of a pump pure-play's current revenue by 2040, and why the price × capacity sensitivity is the one to watch.

    3. Timing points to multiples. In the base case, displacement starts in {{ R.base.summary.first_displacement_year }}. Device equities are valued on long-duration cash flows, so credible human data from an immune-evasive program would likely move terminal-value assumptions years before revenue changes. The catalysts to watch are on the calendar: zimislecel and VX-017 timelines and Sana's SC451 IND.

    What the model leaves out

    • Type 2 diversification. Insulet's Omnipod 5 algorithm received FDA clearance for type 2 diabetes in August 2024 (K241777).1 Growth outside T1D lowers the T1D share over time, which the model holds constant.
    • Competitive shifts. Share moves between pump makers likely matter more to any one company this decade than a cure does.
    • Who sells the cure. Cell therapy revenue accrues to companies like Vertex and Sana, not to device makers.
